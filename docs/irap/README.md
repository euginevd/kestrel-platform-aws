# IRAP assessment pack — Kestrel AWS Landing Zone

> ## ⚠️ THIS IS NOT AN IRAP ASSESSMENT
>
> **The author of this pack is not an IRAP assessor.** Not ASD-endorsed, not registered,
> not qualified to perform an IRAP assessment. This material was generated by an AI
> language model acting in the *role* of an assessor as a writing exercise.
>
> **An IRAP assessment can only be performed by an ASD-endorsed IRAP assessor.** Nothing
> in this folder constitutes an assessment, a certification, an authorisation, or advice
> that a system is fit to hold Australian Government data. It must never be presented to
> an agency, an authorising officer, or a customer as an assessment outcome.
>
> **What this is:** a structured self-critique, written in an assessor's format and voice,
> whose only purpose is to find weak claims before a real assessor does. Treat every
> finding as *a hypothesis to test*, not a determination.
>
> The system assessed — **Kestrel Digital** — is a fictional scenario, and the repository
> is a reference implementation. No real estate exists behind it.

## Purpose of this pack

The [`docs/security/`](../security/) folder contains the documents a system owner submits
to an assessment: the SSP, SRMP, CMP and IRP. Those are written from the *inside*, by the
people who built the system.

This folder is the counterpart, written from the *outside*: what an assessor would make of
those documents and the code behind them. The two are deliberately adversarial. If the
security pack and this pack agree on everything, one of them is not doing its job.

## Framework

Structured against the **[IRAP Common Assessment Framework v1.0 (April 2025)](https://www.cyber.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-04/IRAP%20common%20assessment%20framework.pdf)**,
including its four stages, three assessment methods, evidence quality scale, layering model,
and ASD's mandated implementation-outcome terminology.

Conformance against all 46 IRAP Assessment Requirements (IRAP-AR-0001 to 0046) is stated
requirement by requirement in [09](09-irap-ar-conformance.md): **28 conform, 9 partially
conform, 9 cannot conform** — the last group being those that require a registered assessor,
an assessment team, independent review, or the absence of directed assumptions.

## The three layers

Per the CAF's layering guidance, where a system depends on preceding assessed layers:

| Layer | Who owns it | What is assessed |
|-------|-------------|------------------|
| **1 — AWS** | Amazon Web Services | Not re-assessed. Whether Kestrel's *inheritance claim* is valid — right services, right Regions, current report |
| **2 — Kestrel** | Kestrel Digital | The landing zone as built. **This is the assessment.** |
| **3 — Other entities** | Entra ID, managed SOC, GitHub, CyberArk, tenant workload owners | Assurance position, and whether the seam to Layer 2 is controlled |

Weaknesses cluster in the **seams**, not inside the layers. A control both parties believe
the other owns is the most common serious finding in a cloud assessment, and this pack looks
for those specifically — see [06 §7](06-layer-3-third-parties.md#7-seam-analysis).

## Terminology

ASD's mandated implementation outcomes are used exactly (IRAP-AR-0003). **"Partially
effective" is not an ASD term and does not appear in this pack.**

`Effective` · `Ineffective` · `Alternate control` · `Not assessed` · `Not applicable` ·
`No visibility` · `Not implemented`

**`No visibility`** means adequate assurance could not be obtained. The CAF notes an
authorising officer may treat this as **ineffective from a risk perspective**.

Per IRAP-AR-0034, this pack **articulates potential impact but does not rate risks** on the
entity's behalf — findings carry no severity rating. Per IRAP-AR-0041, it makes **no
statement of compliance, certification or authorisation**.

## Standing assumptions

Two assumptions were directed by the system owner. Both conflict with IRAP-AR-0040 (no
inappropriate assumptions) and IRAP-AR-0023 (assess only what is implemented), and are
declared rather than concealed:

1. **The estate is assumed deployed and operating.** Evidence objects under
   `s3://kestrel-log-archive/irap/phase-<n>/` are assumed to exist. **None were sighted.**
   Every *Effective* outcome means "the declared configuration would meet the control's
   intent if deployed as written" — not "observed operating".
2. **CI/CD workflows are assumed enabled.** They are committed as `*.yml.disabled`. Absent
   this assumption, every pipeline-gated control is **Ineffective**.

## Assessment summary

| | |
|---|---|
| **ISM release** | June 2026 (`2026.06`) |
| **Classification** | PROTECTED |
| **Assessment degree** | Focused |
| **Methods used** | Examine only — **no Interview, no Test** |
| **Evidence ceiling** | **Fair** (copies of configuration) |
| **Controls sampled** | 47 of ~1,101 — judgemental sample |
| **Outcomes** | 21 Effective · 3 Ineffective · 15 No visibility · 1 Not implemented · 1 Alternate control · 2 Not applicable · 4 Not assessed |
| **Weaknesses** | 20 findings · 5 observations |

The design quality is high and materially above what is typical for a scale-up at this
classification. The weaknesses concentrate in **claim precision** — documentation asserting
more than the configuration delivers — and in **assurance asymmetry**: controls Kestrel
implements are evidenced well; controls Kestrel depends on others to implement are asserted.

The **15 `No visibility` outcomes** are the most significant number in the pack. They arise
because two of the CAF's three assessment methods were unavailable, not because controls were
found wanting — but that distinction does not reduce an authorising officer's uncertainty.
